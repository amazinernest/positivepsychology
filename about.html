Who we are

An association built on the science of flourishing

The Positive Psychology Association of Nigeria (PoPAN) is a non-profit organisation established in 2020. Its members are scholars, researchers and practitioners dedicated to the scientific study and the evidence-based, ethical application of positive psychology.

Martin E.P. Seligman introduced and popularised positive psychology, the scientific study of the strengths that enable individuals and communities to thrive. The field rests on the belief that people want to lead meaningful and fulfilling lives, to cultivate what is best within themselves, and to enhance their experiences of love, work and play. Its science operates at three levels: the subjective, the individual and the group.

Many branches of psychology have traditionally concentrated on pathology. Positive psychology builds on the behaviours and traits already present in people. Its interventions aim to boost positive emotions and help people find meaning in life, and it focuses on proactive, intentional, meaningful living that generates happiness and life satisfaction.

Members of PoPAN commonly belong to other professional bodies, among them the Nigerian Psychological Society, the Counselling Association of Nigeria, the Nigerian Society for Educational Psychologists and the Nigerian Association of Clinical Psychologists. The Association’s focus is distinct: positive psychology, and the retraining, professionalism and mentorship that its practice requires.

Our story

From a first meeting to a first conference

  1. 28 June 2020

    Maiden meeting

    The Convener, D.A. Oluwole, PhD, welcomed 41 colleagues to the Association’s first meeting and described PoPAN as a collective project: “It belongs to all of us. I am just a midwife.” Read the address.

  2. November 2020

    Constitution

    The Association’s constitution, dated November 2020, set out its name and emblem, vision, mission, objectives, membership, governance and finances. Read the constitution.

  3. 2022

    First conference planned

    The Association intended to hold its first conference in 2022. A prolonged ASUU strike prevented it.

  4. 19–21 June 2023

    1st Biennial Conference

    PoPAN’s first biennial conference was held at the Faculty of Education Complex, University of Ibadan, under the theme Positive Psychology in Challenging Times. Conference record.

  5. 16–18 June 2025

    2nd Biennial Conference

    The second biennial conference was held at Lagos State University of Education, Epe Campus, under the theme Thriving in a Changing World: Harnessing Resilience and Connection. Conference record.

Vision and mission

Where we are headed, and how

Vision: To integrate, promote and facilitate the science and application of positive psychology in Nigeria and abroad.

Mission

  1. To promote and integrate the science and application of positive psychology towards the advancement of people’s wellbeing, locally and internationally.

  2. To facilitate global collaboration among researchers, teachers, students and practitioners of positive psychology.

  3. To disseminate, and enhance the use of, evidence-based findings of positive psychology with the broadest possible audience.

Aims and objectives

Five objectives

  1. Share knowledge

    Disseminate information on the concepts, principles and strategies of positive psychology, and on how to live a happily fulfilled life.

  2. Practical, evidence-based solutions

    Provide practical, evidence-based solutions to human socio-psychological problems, and assist experts in acquiring new skills.

  3. Counselling services

    Offer e-counselling or face-to-face counselling by experts on mental-health-related issues.

  4. A community of interest

    Bring together people with a common interest to discuss social issues that bear on positive psychology: tips for optimal human functioning and toolkits for making life worth living.

  5. A professional platform

    Provide a platform for promoting academic ideals, job vacancies, fellowship opportunities, scholarships, institutional admissions, professional advancement, publications, conferences, networking and mentoring.

Our emblem

What the emblem represents

Article 2 of the constitution describes the meaning of each element of the Association’s corporate seal.

The bulb
The entire bulb shape represents the human mind and behaviour. The brain is the physical, biological component; behaviour is the mental, psychological aspect.
Carbon black
A barrier between the self and the outside world. It captures human insecurities, defeatism, negativity, lack of self-confidence and other pathologies of the mind.
Eggshell white
Mental alertness, innocence, purity and the desire for change.
Royal blue
The positive mind. Blue is associated with independence, contentment, trustworthiness and reliability.
Dark blue
Elegance, authority and intelligence.
Royal and dark blue together
The combination drives positivity: the right mental and emotional attitude, which facilitates happiness, health and success.
Affiliation and governance

Part of a wider community

PoPAN is an affiliate of the International Positive Psychology Association (IPPA), whose members in over 70 countries are committed to advancing the scientific study and ethical applications of positive psychology.

At home, the Association is governed by its constitution. An Executive Committee, elected by the general body of registered members, manages its affairs and finances and reports to a Board of Trustees at the annual general meeting. Divisions and branches may be formed as the Association grows.
